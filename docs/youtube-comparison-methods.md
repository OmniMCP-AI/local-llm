# Comparison methods from the YouTube research

The imported research includes 30 caption-based video notes, 116 partial hardware profiles, and 450 assorted observations. The observations include creator-reported runs, quoted prices, third-party results and projections. The same setup can support different conclusions depending on the model, workload, and ownership assumptions. Each method below identifies the decision, the source example, and how fastest.ai can present it. [Research inventory](Local-AI-Comparison-Platform/YouTube-Local-LLM-Transcripts/README.md)

The source column describes what the imported notes establish. The visualization column specifies how to apply the method, including extensions where the source lacks a complete dataset. Video timestamps come from the archived notes. They are references to the original recordings, with no independent audio or measurement verification in this review.

## Shared calculation and evidence rules

- **Acquisition.** GPU-only spend sums priced cards at their required quantities. A build adds other components once and records quotes, paid prices, bundles, reuse, omissions, and unknowns explicitly. An upgrade uses incremental cash outlay. A total sourced from a video stays visible as that creator's estimate if the detailed parts cannot substantiate it. Market, date, item condition, tax treatment and currency carry through to the chart.
- **Operation.** Monthly IT electricity is the sum of system wall watts times hours in each phase, divided by 1,000, and multiplied by the electricity tariff. Add externally metered cooling, or apply a disclosed cooling estimate to the same boundary. Account for maintenance, fees or upgrades as separate cash flows. Whole-system wall power already includes internal fans and other devices in that boundary. A GPU power limit alone cannot estimate total system energy.
- **Capacity and equivalence.** Model the delivered workload from actual volume, input/output mix, concurrency, observed service times, operating windows, and quality requirements. A simple throughput-based ceiling is a projection. Keep local release/quantization, provider endpoint/version, tokenizer, caching, output quality, and deployment constraints visible. Buyers can compare costs for a workload class even when equivalent delivery remains unverified, with payback qualified accordingly.
- **Ownership and payback.** For a fixed operating scenario, divide upfront investment by positive monthly avoided cloud spend after local operating costs. A nonpositive saving produces no payback. For time-varying use, compute each alternative's cumulative cash flows and identify the first crossing in the modeled horizon. Resale, discount rate, downtime and price changes are explicit optional assumptions. Subsequent cash flows can reverse a crossover.
- **Publication.** Store source media ID, timestamp, run or quote ID, provenance class, equipment revision, method version, and exact comparison conditions with each point. Show human-readable values and downloadable evidence, preserve ranges and missingness, and give animation controls and equivalent static views. A production comparison needs attributable inputs and compatibility checks. Illustrative prototype values should carry a synthetic or scenario label.
